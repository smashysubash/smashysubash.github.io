Our infrastructure had grown across three providers: AWS, Azure and DigitalOcean. Each new client, service and experiment added a machine somewhere. Nobody had a single view of all of it, and nobody had asked me to look.

I looked anyway. The monthly bill is now 35% lower.

## Step 1: see everything before touching anything

You can't right-size what you can't see. Each provider has its own console and its own metrics, and none of them show the others.

So the first thing I did was deploy Beszel, a lightweight monitoring agent, on every machine across all three clouds. For the first time we had one dashboard with memory, disk and CPU for the whole fleet.

That view changed the conversation. We could finally see which machines cost money and what each one actually used.

## Step 2: decide what "too big" means

Most of our machines were burstable instances. On those, CPU is a misleading signal: it spikes, it idles, and the credits smooth it out. So I set CPU aside and made decisions on the two things that actually run out:

- **Memory.** A machine sitting below 40% memory use was a candidate to shrink.
- **Disk.** A disk close to full is an outage waiting to happen, whatever the bill says.

I also set one rule to keep risk low: **only halve, never jump.** 8 GB could go to 4 GB, and 4 GB to 2 GB, but nothing dropped two sizes at once. If a service needs more after a change, the step back is small and obvious.

## Step 3: the audit

With the data in one place, I went through every resource, one by one, against what it actually used.

The result split into three groups:

1. **Downsize.** Machines that had been sized for a load that never came. These were the savings.
2. **Upsize.** A few machines were running at around 90% memory, one traffic spike from falling over. The goal was always matching size to need, and sometimes that means more.
3. **Fix the disks.** Some machines were above 90% disk. They got a clean-up and more space before they became an incident.

## The result

The monthly cloud bill dropped 35%. The machines we grew are more stable, and the near-full disks are no longer a risk.

## What I'd tell anyone doing this

- **Visibility first.** One dashboard across every provider did more than any single change.
- **Use the right signal.** On burstable instances, memory and disk tell you more than CPU.
- **Small, reversible steps.** Halving is easy to undo. Big jumps are not.
- **Right-size in both directions.** Some machines need more, and finding them is part of the job.
